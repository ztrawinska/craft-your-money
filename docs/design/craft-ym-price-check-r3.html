Still one framed surface

The review is an inset inside the pricing block, not a card floating over it — an iris left-rule and a barely-there iris wash. The clay frame stays the screen's only enclosure, so the r3 rule holds even when the assistant speaks.

Iris earns its place

Iris appears on exactly four things: the glint, the section label, the finding numerals, and the follow-up chips. It never touches the price, the profit, or the status chip — those stay clay and status colours. Iris marks who is speaking, never what the answer is.

The assistant reviews, never decides

Scenarios are preview-only — they show what a different price would mean, but tapping one never sets it. There is no "apply" anywhere in this card. The price stays the user's decision.

Provenance is part of the answer

Used / Assumed / Can't know closes every review. It states what the assistant actually had, and — more importantly — admits what it cannot know (demand, what buyers will pay). Honesty about limits is what makes the rest trustworthy.

Bounded, not a chat

Follow-ups are chips, never free text, and they're generated from the findings themselves — finding 1 mentions labour, so "Why so labour-heavy?" exists. Roughly three exchanges deep, then it stops. The assistant cannot introduce new UI.

Silent by default

None of this exists until the user taps the glint. One protective exception: activating below the calculated price offers a declinable second look. The assistant never opens on its own.
